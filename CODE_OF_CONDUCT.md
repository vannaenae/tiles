# Code of Conduct

Tiles is a SOULSPCE Collective product, built in the open by people who give their time to it. We want everyone who takes part to feel welcome and safe.

This code applies in every Tiles project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, commit messages, and anywhere you speak for the project. It also applies to behaviour elsewhere that harms people in the project.

## What we expect

- **Be kind.** Treat people the way you'd want to be treated, especially when you disagree.
- **Assume good faith.** Most mistakes are honest ones. Ask before you accuse.
- **Keep it about the work.** Criticise ideas and code, never the person.
- **Make room for others.** Welcome newcomers, explain things plainly, and don't talk over people.
- **Respect people's time and boundaries.** If someone says no or asks you to stop, stop.
- **Own your mistakes.** Apologise, learn from it and move on.

## What's not OK

- Harassment, threats or intimidation, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs, or jokes at someone's expense, including about their identity, background, body, ability or beliefs.
- Sexual language, images or attention.
- Sharing someone's private information without their clear permission.
- Repeatedly disrupting conversations or derailing them on purpose.
- Encouraging or helping anyone else to do any of these things.

## How to report

If something happens that breaks this code, or makes you feel unsafe, please tell us.

- Email [contact email], or
- message a maintainer privately on GitHub, or use GitHub's **Report content** option on the comment or issue.

Tell us what happened, where, and who was involved. Links or screenshots help. You can report something that happened to someone else.

We'll keep reports as private as we can. We'll only share details with people who need them to deal with the report. We won't tolerate anyone treating you badly for reporting in good faith.

## What the maintainers will do

The maintainers look after this code. When they get a report, they will review it, listen to the people involved, and decide what to do. Depending on what happened, they may:

- talk to the person privately and ask them to change how they act;
- edit, hide or delete comments, issues, pull requests or other contributions;
- give a formal warning;
- block someone from the project for a while, or for good.

Maintainers who break this code are held to it the same as anyone else. If a report involves a maintainer, that maintainer stays out of handling it.

## Questions

If you're not sure whether something is OK, ask a maintainer. We'd much rather you asked.
